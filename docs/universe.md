# The Long Night: one broken day, three people, one thread

```
./.venv/bin/python -m echo --game=night        # The Long Night: all three, one thread
./.venv/bin/python -m echo                     # Echo Delve on its own (Aurel, 10 floors)
./.venv/bin/python -m echo --game=stillwater   # Stillwater on its own (Tarn)
./.venv/bin/python -m echo --game=tick         # The Tick on its own (Maren)
```

## The world

**The Loom of Hours**, at the bottom of the Deep, weaves each day and sends it up to Hollowmere along
one golden thread. Nobody knows who made the Loom, or whether anyone did. It just is. **The thread is time itself**: every hour the town will ever have.

**It used to be thick as a wrist.** Once the day came up as a golden cord you could barely close a hand
around, and the days were long. For generations the Guild of Hours shaved a little off every day (a
minute here, an hour there), wound it onto the drum of their great clock, the Tick, and sold it back as
warmth and lamplight. The days grew short (the oldest people in Hollowmere remember summer evenings
that lasted until midnight). It wore down to a thread.

**Where the thread runs.** Not up the open stairs: it climbs through **the Spindle**, a bore as wide as
a chimney (cut when the thread was thick), sealed inside the rock, from the Loom's hall at the bottom of the Deep to
the Guild's drum at the top. Sealed in stone, it never let out a glimmer. Beside it winds **the
Spindle stair**, built to tend it; its sconces are what the Vells, Hollowmere's lamplighters, have kept
lit for generations. Halfway up, only a thin wall of limestone lies between the Spindle and caves that
nobody in Hollowmere knew were there: the caves of the Still.

```
          [ the Tick: the Guild's drum ]        (Hollowmere, above)
                       ||
   the Spindle   ======||
   stair           ====||
  (Aurel)      ========||                     the caves of the Still
                   ====||  <-- the break -->  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
               ========||   (a thin wall,        (Tarn)
                   ====||    broken open)
               ========||
                       ||   <- the Spindle: a chimney-wide bore, sealed in rock
              [ the Loom's hall ]  (at the very bottom, far below the caves and walled off)
```

**It snapped where it wore thinnest**: halfway up, where it had rubbed for years against a crack in the
Spindle's wall, right beside the Still's caves. 312 days ago, carrying a day:

- **The top half whipped up to the drum**, and the Guild kept what it held: the *hoard*. Its broken
  end fell back out of the Spindle onto the stair's first landing, where Aurel finds it. That end is
  still wound onto the drum, so every hour the Guild lets go runs down to him as slack.
- **The lower half fell slack** down the Spindle to the Loom's hall. That's why Aurel has to go all the
  way down the stair, lighting the sconces as he goes, to tie the two ends.
- **The rest of the day burst through the thin wall, broke it open**, and poured into the Still's black
  water, where it settled like silt. Still water looks black; stir it and the light rises in rings.
- **The Still had never had light.** The thread ran past them sealed in stone their whole lives, and
  the Loom's own light is at the very bottom of the Deep, far below them and walled off.
- **The hole the day broke is the Still's first way out in generations**: big enough to climb through
  (the bore is wide), into the Spindle. The Spindle is smooth and sheer, so there is only
  one way to go: **down**, a long slide, into the Loom's hall. From there the only way on is the
  Spindle stair, up, by the lamps Aurel lit on his way down.
- **The keeper's windows.** When the Guild began drawing the thread up, they cut barred windows from
  the stair into the Spindle to watch it run. On Aurel's floor 5 (the Cistern of Echoes) one looks
  across the bore, through the break in its far wall, at a black pool where rings of light spread and
  fade: the Still, moving. That's where the stories first meet.

### How time works: three rules

Everything else (the Guild's business, the hoard, why the Still lose their light, why the Guild keeps
the night) follows from three rules, told in the Long Night's opening, one per panel.

1. **There is only ever one day.** The Loom weaves a day and sends it up the thread. Every night it
   unpicks the day that has ended, and by morning it has woven the *same thread* into the next. Days
   are not used up by being lived; they are rewoven. (Penelope's loom, run every night.)
2. **Thread burned in a lamp never goes back to the Loom.** That is the one way to destroy it.
3. **A day is a great deal of light.** A single day lights and warms a whole town and its sky.

**How the Guild got rich, and the days got short.** Once the thread was thick as a wrist and summer
evenings lasted until midnight. It began as a kindness: the Guild kept a little of each long summer
day back on the drum of their great clock (the Tick keeps time, literally) to warm the winter
nights. But every lampful they sold was thread that would never go back to the Loom, so each morning
was woven from a little less. The shorter the days grew, the more lamps the town needed, and the more
the Guild sold. The thread wore thin, and snapped with a whole day on it.

**The night since the snap.** The Loom has had nothing to unpick, so nothing is rewoven: every minute
that passes now spends thread for good. What is left of it is all the time there is. Half the last
day was caught on the Guild's drum; sold a lampful at a time it lasts for years, and the price of an
hour goes up every night. The Guild doesn't need to make more. They own the only light there is.

**Why the Guild won't let the thread be tied.** Tie it, and the Loom can weave again, and it takes back
everything left of the old day to make the morning (rule 1): the hoard on the drum, the light in the
Still's water, hours kept in a watch. The morning would be free, and their hoard gone. They have done
the arithmetic: there is enough on the drum to keep *them* warm for the rest of their lives. Whoever
holds the only hours decides what happens in them, so they would rather keep the night.

**The thread is the time that is left.** It shortens with every minute and lengthens only when the
Guild's hours are let go back into it. If it runs out before it is tied, there is nothing left for the
Loom to weave a morning from.

**And what is left is all there will ever be.** Burned thread never comes back, so when the thread is
tied, the Loom can only weave days as long as what survived the night: the thread, and whatever of the
hoard was not sold. That is why there is night at all: a day lasts as long as its thread, and the rest
is dark. The summer evenings that lasted until midnight were a thicker thread.

**How long the first day is.** It is a *share* of a whole summer day as the old people remember them
(dawn at six to midnight): the share of the last day that survived the night (the thread left, the drum,
Maren's watch) out of what there was at nightfall (the whole thread, the drum, the watch). A careful night
makes the longest day anyone in Hollowmere can remember, until late afternoon. It never reaches midnight
again: that would need back every hour the Guild ever burned. A night that scraped through makes a day
just long enough to see the sun come up.

**How it stays ended.** Nothing physical stops the Guild from closing the brake and starting again.
Walls can be dug through; the Spindle was bored once, it could be bored again. What stops it is that
the town finally understands. The Guild's trade worked because it took generations: nobody alive
remembered the days being long, so nobody connected the short days to the lamps. The Guild was
selling a cure for a cold it was making. Maren takes the proof with her from a clerk's desk on her
second floor (the ledger: every hour kept back, every day, for generations, and what it sold for).
If she lets the hoard go, she nails the ledgers to the door of the Tick on the first morning, and
nobody in Hollowmere pays for an hour again; the brake is left open and rusts that way. If she takes
the Guild's bargain (or never reaches the drum), nobody tells the town, and by nightfall the brake is
closed again and the Guild has quietly begun to keep a little of every day back. Her choice is the
difference between one morning and all the mornings after it.

**Tarn's choice, at the break.** When Tarn brings his people to the break he decides: take them
through, or let them stay in the last of the light. Through is a bet on the others: they slide down
the Spindle into the Loom's hall (landing in a heap), and if Aurel lights the stair and ties the thread
they climb into the sun; if not, they are stranded at the bottom of the world in the dark, without even
the water. Staying is the hedge: if no morning ever comes, the light never leaves their water (the
water dims as the thread shortens, but never goes out); if it does come, the water goes black around
them. What Tarn sees through the break depends on Aurel: lamps at the bottom of the stair if he has
come that far down, or only dark.

**How Maren knows it is done.** If she let the hoard go, it ran down the thread to whoever held the
other end. When Aurel ties it, the drum shudders and turns the other way, the Loom pulling, and the
watch in her hand, which has run backwards all night, ticks forwards for the first time.

## The family

**The lantern passes from mother to daughter.** When the dark first came up, the Vells' mother took
it down to find the break and never came back. She is a Warden in the Deep now, pacing before the
Loom's door with her lantern.

- **Maren** should have carried it down next. She had seen what the stair does to people. On the
  doorstep she put the lantern into her brother's hands, said she was sorry, and ran, telling
  herself he would never be fool enough to go down.
- **Aurel**, who was never taught the stair, went down in her place.

**Why Maren runs, and why she can steal the watch.** Not cowardice exactly: fear, and grief, and the
certainty that nobody comes back from the Deep. In a town without mornings, the only people hiring were
the people selling the light, so for a year she has kept the Guild's books in the Counting House, a
minute at a time, and watched the Guildmaster take out a pocket watch whenever a delivery ran late and
turn the whole tower back. That is how she knows what the Regulator is. The theft is planned, for
months: tonight, when the last clerk has gone home, she takes it from his desk, meaning to leave
Hollowmere and carry her own day with her. What she did not know is that the watch is tied to the bell.
It rings; the doors below are barred; the only way out of the Tick is up, through the works, past the
Drum Room, to the roof, and down the old lamplighters' ladder on the outside of the tower, unused since
the Guild took the lamps. The game is her escape.

**Who the lamplighters are.** For as long as there has been a stair down to the Loom, a Vell has walked
it every evening lighting the sconces, so that whoever had to go down to tend the thread could find the
way. Once the Guild took the thread (and the town's lamps, and the clock's), nobody went down any more.
The Vells lit the stair anyway.

**What Maren learns, and when.** She doesn't know Aurel went down, or that her hours matter to him.
She finds out on the way up (cards after each floor, and two interludes):

1. The watch ticks against her pulse. Nobody comes back from the Deep: this is all the time there is.
2. A Guild ledger: *"The broken end has been taken up from the first landing. It is going down the
   stair. Watch the drum."*
3. *(Interlude)* From behind a clock face she sees the mouth of the Deep, and the first sconce on the
   stair burning again. Nobody has lit it since their mother went down. She doesn't look at it again.
4. The drum's axle shudders and turns a notch the wrong way. Something far below is pulling.
5. The wardens walk faster. The Guild feels every tug on the thread, and they are afraid.
6. *(Interlude)* The Drum Room: the last day wound on the drum, the thread running down through the
   floor, twitching. The Guildmaster is waiting by the brake, and he doesn't call the wardens.

**The Guild's bargain.** Keeping the hoard for herself was never a real temptation: the morning takes it
back, so only a selfish player would choose it. The Guild knows better than to offer her money. It
offers her her brother. *"You don't know what you're holding. Open that, and the thread will take
every hour of it, and your brother with it. It has a way of tying people down. Leave the brake to us.
We will send him what he needs to tie it, and not a minute more for him to lose himself in. He comes
home. And at dawn we open the drum ourselves: no more keeping back. You have our word. You keep the
watch, and go wherever you like, warm. All we ask for is the ledger."*

Four promises. They keep the first two (the slack, and Aurel home). The third is a lie by omission:
the morning takes the watch back, and they knew it would. The fourth is the one that matters, and by
nightfall they have broken it.

The bargain is real, which is what makes it a choice (in The Long Night):

- **Open the brake** (trust herself): the drum's remaining hoard and her watch run down the thread,
  the Guild stops selling, and on the first morning she is waiting at the mouth of the Deep, takes the
  lantern at last, and nails the ledgers to the door of the Tick.
- **Take his bargain**: a fixed length (8% of a perfect night) goes down the thread, and the Guild pays
  for every echo Aurel unravels from then on, so he cannot be woven in. She keeps the watch and leaves
  in a Guild coach; the morning takes the watch back on the road anyway. The Guild keeps the drum and
  goes on selling it, and keeps the ledger (it is ash by nightfall): the drum is never opened at dawn,
  and by nightfall the brake is shut again.

Late to the drum (the Guild has sold most of it), or with Aurel struggling, the bargain can be worth
more minutes than the brake. It is never worth the mornings after.

## Each of them has their own chapters

Every story opens with its own **prologue**, told from inside that person's head, and has
**interludes** along the way (in each game on its own, and in The Long Night):

| | Prologue | Interludes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurel** | the lantern, the doorstep, the binding on the first landing, the stair | floor 5: resting halfway, understanding Maren now; floor 10: his mother, a Warden at the Loom's door |
| **Maren** | the doorstep from her side, a year keeping the Guild's books, the watch, the theft, the bell | floor 3: the first sconce lit again; the Drum Room: the choice coming |
| **Tarn** | the Still, the light in the water, Tarn, the light thinning | the first chamber past the tutorial: the old ones and the children; the last: the break, and **Tarn's choice** |

In The Long Night the world's own story comes first (the Loom, the Guild, the Spindle in section,
the snap, the thread as time, the three of them on one night), and each prologue is told the first
time you play that person's part. The night ends with a scene for each of them (how it went for
Aurel, for the Still, for Maren), then the record.

## The three stories

| | Where | Who | Mechanic | What it spends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Echo Delve** | Within: the Deep | **Aurel**, the lamplighter's son | his failed loops replay as echoes | the last day (and his thread) |
| **The Tick** | Above: the Guild's tower | **Maren**, the lamplighter's daughter | the **Regulator**, a stolen pocket watch, winds the tower back around her | the last day, and the **hoard** |
| **Stillwater** | Below: the caves | **Tarn**, one of the Still | stir the spilled light; your people follow your wake | the last day |

### Why a pocket watch

The Tick is the tower; the **Regulator** is how the Guild spends what the tower holds. Whoever holds
it can turn the tower's hours back around themselves, wardens and gears and all, while they stay put.
Maren steals it in her prologue; the floors are her way out. Every tick she winds back spends a minute
of the hoard.

### The Tick is about selfishness

Maren uses the hoard to escape. Nothing stops her spending all of it: she can clear every floor by
winding back freely. But the hoard is what's left for everyone else.

- In the **Drum Room** at the top, she chooses: **open the brake** (everything left runs down the thread,
  to Aurel and the Still), or take the **Guild's bargain** (see above).
- Hours kept in her watch **go back to the Loom when the new day comes** (rule 1): the watch goes cold
  in her hand on the road out of town. She only learns that at the end.

### Echo Delve is about the duty she gave away

Aurel, untrained, carries a lantern that was never meant for him, lighting the sconces on the way
down, to the Warden on the last stair who carries his mother's lantern.

**The lamps are a mechanic, not just set dressing.** Every floor has sconces along the stair. Walking
close to one (within a couple of tiles, with nothing solid in between) lights it; lit sconces stay
lit across echoes and show the floor around them. In the Long Night the lamps are also the Still's way
home: the share of sconces Aurel lit is the share of the stair they can see to climb, so a dark stair
costs some of the Still Tarn saved.

**Par follows the sconces.** Lighting them takes turns the fastest clear doesn't, so a single par would
punish the completionist. Instead every floor has a *par curve*, planned in the same clean room as par
(enemies aside) and checked by replaying it keystroke for keystroke: the fastest clear (with whatever
sconces it passes anyway), rising by what each further sconce costs, cheapest first, up to the clear
that lights every one. Your grade is measured against the par for the number you lit, and each sconce
also adds to the score, so **every sconce at par is the best score on a floor** (S+, and about 1,200
points), while the fastest clear at par is still an S+ (about 1,100). Every sconce is placed where that
plan can reach it, sometimes with a spare echo loop. Lighting all of them typically costs 10 to 50%
more turns than the fastest clear, because the waits in the last loop absorb a lot of the detours.

### Stillwater is about getting out in time

The Still must be out before the new day takes the light from their water. The thing that saves the
town (Aurel tying the thread) is the thing that ends their light.

**The line is the difficulty.** The Still never crowd onto one another's tile, so the more of them follow
you, the longer the line. Each can only step where they can see (your wake: the water your ripples lit,
and the tiles you walked through, for a few turns) or beside someone ahead of them in the line who can:
the old ones walk with a hand on the shoulder in front, and so does everyone. A long line therefore
holds together, but it is long: more of them wading means more ripples for the eels to feel, a float-gate
has to stay up long enough for all of them to pass single file, and you have to walk on past a gap to
make room for the ones behind. (Float-gates stay up 12 turns for this.)

**If they came down before Aurel.** When Aurel reaches the Loom's door and Tarn has already brought the
Still down the Spindle, they are there, sitting close together off to the side, and the Long Night (and
the Story) gives them a scene of their own: one of them reaches out, very slowly, and touches his hand.

## The Long Night: the core loop

**The thread is the shared resource, and it is time.** Every turn in any of the three stories spends
a minute of it. The night is built first, then you choose how much thread is left, shown as real
time for that night: a multiple of *this night's perfect par*, the fewest minutes in which every
sconce on Aurel's stair is lit, every one of the Still is brought through, and Maren gets out.

- A long thread: 2.2× perfect par. Enough to do everything, with room for the fog and for mistakes.
- A measured thread: 1.7×. Enough for everything, if you are careful.
- A short thread: 1.35×. Someone will be left behind, or some of the stair left dark.

So a completionist always has a length where the perfect ending is within reach, and the shorter
ones are where the choices (minutes against lamps, the Still against the stair) start to bite.

**How each story touches the thread:**

| | Shortens it | Lengthens it |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurel** | every turn; **each unravel cuts 20 minutes** (his slack *is* the thread) | — |
| **Maren** | every turn (winding back spends the Guild's hoard, not the thread) | **every hour reel she cuts loose** unspools into it (+8); **releasing the hoard** at the top |
| **Tarn** | every turn | — |

- **Maren's selfishness is now measurable.** Every minute she winds back for herself is a minute of
  the Guild's hoard that never goes back into the thread. The hoard she reaches the top with is
  what she can give. Kept hours go back to the Loom when the new day comes.
- **The water dims as the thread shortens.** A chamber is played at a light level between 55% and
  100%, set by how much thread is left when Tarn enters it. Every ripple's reach and the wake's
  length scale with it: later chambers are darker, the Still lose sight of you sooner, and
  one pebble may no longer reach every eel.
  - Generated chambers are checked at the dimmest light, so they stay possible.
  - The third tutorial chamber needs both its pebbles when the water is dim.
- **The opening** is fixed (Aurel, then Maren, then Tarn, each with their prologue); after that, any
  order, and the order is a real decision (see *What the order changes*). You can leave a
  level part-way (minutes spent stay spent; Aurel resumes where he left off).
- **A mistake in the water costs time, not the chamber.** If an eel finds Tarn, he can step back a few
  turns (the turns stay spent) instead of starting the chamber over.
- **Aurel ends the night.** When he ties the thread on his last floor, the new day comes for
  everyone, ready or not. Any of the Still not yet out are lost. The game warns you first.
- **If the thread runs out** before it is tied, there is no morning at all.
- **If the thread is too short for Aurel to unravel** when his echo slots are full, the Deep weaves
  him in.

**The tensions:**

- Every minute Aurel spends exploring is a minute the Still don't get, and darker water for them.
- But every sconce he leaves cold is a dark stretch of the stair the Still must climb later.
- Every minute Maren winds back is a minute that never returns to the thread.
- Aurel can't finish until Tarn is out.
- Tarn wants to go early, while the water is bright.

## What the order changes: the main decision

Every minute of the night burns thread for good, and **whatever thread is left when Aurel ties it
becomes the morning**: the first day lasts from dawn for as many hours as survived (shown on the hub:
"Tied now, it would make a day until 4:30 in the afternoon"). Nothing else makes more. So after the
opening, choosing whose part to play next is the main strategic decision, because order changes how
much thread the night costs:

- **Maren early.** The Guild sells the drum all night, a lampful at a time, and every lampful is burned
  for good. They start with 30% of a perfect night on the drum, sell 0.2 minutes of it for every
  minute that passes, and sell faster (+25% per floor) the deeper Aurel goes, because they can feel him
  coming. Whatever is left when Maren reaches the Drum Room is hers to let go into the thread (or trade
  for the Guild's bargain, which is worth a fixed amount: late to the drum, it can be worth more).
- **Tarn early.** In dim water the Still move slowly, feeling for each other's hands: every turn costs
  1/light minutes (1 at full light, about 1.8 at the dimmest). The water dims as the thread shortens.
- **Aurel's sconces.** Each one he lights shines through the keeper's windows and brightens Tarn's water
  (up to +20% light at a fully lit stair), which makes Tarn's turns cheaper too. But every floor Aurel
  clears makes the Guild sell faster, and he is the biggest spender of thread of the three.
- **Aurel last, always.** Tying the thread ends the night. Any of the Still still in the caves are lost;
  whatever is left on the drum, or in Maren's watch, goes back to the Loom with the rest.

Measured on one night, with every level played at 1.3× par and every sconce lit: Maren, then Tarn,
then Aurel leaves about 12½ hours of thread on a measured thread; Aurel first leaves about 8. On a
short thread the same choice is the difference between a morning that lasts until 11:30 and one that
is over by 6:36.

And the ending answers for what you neglected: a scene for the Still (sunrise, or black water), for
Maren (waiting for Aurel at the mouth of the Deep, where this time she takes the lantern; or alone on the
roof of the Tick with a watch gone cold), and for Aurel.

## Hooks between the stories (already in the text)

- **Echo Delve, floor 5:** through a keeper's window and the break, rings of light on black water: the
  Still. (In The Long Night, if Tarn has already led them out, the water is quite still.)
- **Echo Delve, floor 7:** the Tick strikes an hour it should not: Maren spending the hoard, above.
- **Echo Delve, floor 9:** beyond a keeper's window, something pale drops past in the dark of the
  Spindle, and then another: the Still, sliding down to the Loom.
- **Maren's interlude:** the first sconce of the stair, lit again. She knows who lit it.
- **Endings:** the Still sliding down into the Loom's hall, and climbing Aurel's lit stair behind him
  into their first sunrise; Maren, if she let the hoard go, waiting at the mouth of the Deep. He holds
  out their mother's lantern, and this time she takes it (the doorstep, answered).
